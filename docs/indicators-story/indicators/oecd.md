# OECD procurement integrity framework

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Institution | Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) |
| Type | International intergovernmental framework |
| Primary use | Risk-management principles, bid-rigging/collusion prevention and detection, and procurement governance |
| Geographic scope | International; principles require adaptation to Lithuanian and EU law |

An OECD warning sign is a reason to review and, where warranted, report a procurement. It is not proof of collusion, corruption, fraud, or illegality.

## Sources and coding

- [Recommendation of the Council on Public Procurement, OECD/LEGAL/0411](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/320/body-text.en.html)
- [Recommendation of the Council on Fighting Bid Rigging in Public Procurement, OECD/LEGAL/0396](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/284)
- [OECD Guidelines for Fighting Bid Rigging in Public Procurement, 2025 Update](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-fighting-bid-rigging-in-public-procurement-2025-update_cbe05a56-en.html)

The OECD sources do not assign machine-oriented codes to every principle and warning sign. `OECD-GOV`, `OECD-TD`, and `OECD-BR` are catalogue codes assigned here in source order.

## Governance and risk-management principles (12)

| Code | Principle | Catalogue interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| OECD-GOV-01 | Transparency | Make procurement rules, opportunities, awards, performance, and public-fund flows visible and usable |
| OECD-GOV-02 | Integrity | Apply integrity standards, conflict controls, training, internal controls, and supplier anti-corruption measures |
| OECD-GOV-03 | Access | Facilitate access to procurement opportunities for competitors of all sizes |
| OECD-GOV-04 | Balance | Balance secondary policy objectives against the primary procurement objective |
| OECD-GOV-05 | Stakeholder participation | Use transparent consultation, supplier dialogue, and external scrutiny with safeguards |
| OECD-GOV-06 | Efficiency | Streamline procurement processes and reduce duplication and waste |
| OECD-GOV-07 | E-procurement | Use integrated, secure digital procurement throughout the cycle |
| OECD-GOV-08 | Capacity | Maintain a professional, adequately skilled procurement workforce |
| OECD-GOV-09 | Evaluation | Measure procurement performance, effectiveness, and savings using reliable information |
| OECD-GOV-10 | Risk management | Map, detect, mitigate, and publicize risks throughout the procurement cycle |
| OECD-GOV-11 | Accountability | Apply oversight, clear responsibility, complaint mechanisms, and proportionate sanctions |
| OECD-GOV-12 | Integration | Integrate procurement with public-finance management and service-delivery systems |

`OECD-GOV-10` specifically supports risk-assessment tools, red-flag systems, whistleblowing arrangements, intervention points, and stakeholder awareness.

## Pro-competitive tender-design controls (6)

| Code | Tender-design control |
|---|---|
| OECD-TD-01 | Understand needs, markets, suppliers, prices, past tenders, and collusion history before designing the tender |
| OECD-TD-02 | Maximize participation by genuinely competing bidders using proportionate and non-discriminatory conditions |
| OECD-TD-03 | Define requirements clearly while avoiding predictable procurement patterns that facilitate market allocation |
| OECD-TD-04 | Reduce communication among bidders and disclose procurement information carefully |
| OECD-TD-05 | Choose evaluation and award criteria that protect current and future competition |
| OECD-TD-06 | Require independent-bid attestations and explain sanctions, leniency, complaints, and whistleblowing channels |

## Bid-rigging and collusion warning signs (48)

The warning signs below are normalized from sections 3.1–3.5 of the OECD's 2025 Bid-Rigging Detection List.

### Bidding patterns

| Code | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| OECD-BR-01 | Same supplier is often the successful bidder |
| OECD-BR-02 | Winning tenders appear geographically allocated |
| OECD-BR-03 | Expected regular supplier does not bid but later subcontracts or bids elsewhere |
| OECD-BR-04 | Suppliers unexpectedly or frequently withdraw submitted bids |
| OECD-BR-05 | Certain companies consistently bid but never win |
| OECD-BR-06 | Companies appear to take turns winning |
| OECD-BR-07 | Companies submit a joint bid despite previously competing independently, or one performs the work |
| OECD-BR-08 | Consistent group submits incomplete or non-responsive bids |

### Tender-document patterns

| Code | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| OECD-BR-09 | Different bidders make identical errors or fail to correct them |
| OECD-BR-10 | Bids have identical or similar content, format, metadata, IP address, author, or creation time |
| OECD-BR-11 | Same person submits bids for competitors |
| OECD-BR-12 | One bid refers to a competitor's bid or uses another bidder's contact or document details |
| OECD-BR-13 | Usually capable bidder submits an unexpectedly incomplete or erroneous bid |
| OECD-BR-14 | Different bids contain identical item-cost estimates or ancillary terms |
| OECD-BR-15 | Bids show matching adjustments |
| OECD-BR-16 | Bids lack expected detail or otherwise appear non-genuine |
| OECD-BR-17 | Competing bids are submitted together or within an unusually short interval |
| OECD-BR-18 | Competitors repeatedly submit bids in the same sequence |
| OECD-BR-19 | Bidders share address, office, intermediary, or financial obligations |

### Pricing patterns

| Code | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| OECD-BR-20 | Unexplained sudden or identical bid-price increases |
| OECD-BR-21 | Expected price discounts disappear or decrease significantly |
| OECD-BR-22 | Discounts are below those usually offered in the same market |
| OECD-BR-23 | Prices remain unchanged despite changes in market or tender terms |
| OECD-BR-24 | Prices become identical after previously differing |
| OECD-BR-25 | Bid-price differences are regular, repetitive, or follow a mathematical formula |
| OECD-BR-26 | Large winner-to-other-bid gap or clustering of losing bids |
| OECD-BR-27 | Supplier bids much higher than for a comparable contract |
| OECD-BR-28 | Winning bid substantially exceeds estimated value |
| OECD-BR-29 | Prices fall significantly after entry by a new or infrequent supplier |
| OECD-BR-30 | Local supplier charges more for local delivery than distant delivery |
| OECD-BR-31 | Local and non-local suppliers quote similar transportation costs |
| OECD-BR-32 | Only one bidder contacts wholesalers for pricing before submission |

### Suspicious statements

| Code | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| OECD-BR-33 | Spoken or written reference to an agreement among bidders |
| OECD-BR-34 | Reference to industry-suggested, standard, scheduled, or “fair” prices |
| OECD-BR-35 | Statement that a company does not sell in a particular area or to particular customers |
| OECD-BR-36 | Statement that an area or customer belongs to another supplier |
| OECD-BR-37 | Advance knowledge of competitors' bids, prices, or unpublished outcome |
| OECD-BR-38 | Statement indicating cover bidding |
| OECD-BR-39 | Different suppliers use identical language to explain price increases |
| OECD-BR-40 | Bidder questions or dismisses independent-bid certificates |
| OECD-BR-41 | Reference to relevant discussions within a trade association |

### Suspicious behaviour

| Code | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| OECD-BR-42 | Suppliers meet before submitting bids |
| OECD-BR-43 | Suppliers regularly socialize or hold regular meetings |
| OECD-BR-44 | Company requests tender information for itself and competitors |
| OECD-BR-45 | Company submits its own bid and a competitor's bid |
| OECD-BR-46 | Bid is submitted by a company incapable of performing the contract |
| OECD-BR-47 | Several bidders use the same consultant or make similar enquiries |
| OECD-BR-48 | Winner subcontracts to unsuccessful bidder or rejects contract and becomes subcontractor |

## Detection and escalation controls

The OECD recommends preserving bid and procurement records, documenting suspicious events promptly, reviewing tender histories for patterns, protecting inquiry confidentiality, and consulting the competent competition authority when serious concerns arise. Cooperation among procurement, competition, audit, anti-corruption, police, and prosecution authorities is part of the framework.
